# Voice preservation

The point of editing is a clearer version of the same person, not a cleaner version of nobody.

## Find the voice before touching anything

Read the whole draft and name three to five signals. Candidates:

- Vocabulary: plain, technical, slangy, formal, regional.
- Cadence: long spoken sentences, short punches, a mix.
- Bluntness: hedged, direct, combative.
- Humor: dry, self-deprecating, none.
- Uncertainty: "I think," "maybe," admitted gaps.
- Digressions: asides and stories that carry character.
- Polish: rough and fast, or careful.

Those signals are the things the edit must survive.

## Keep

- Strong opinions, blunt lines, profanity, humor, self-interruptions, honest admissions, when they belong to the writer.
- Fragments and long spoken sentences when they are clear and characteristic.
- Personal asides and stories that create context, tension, or character.
- Hedges that express real uncertainty.
- The writer's progression and detours when they carry personality.
- The specific fact. Never smooth "cut review time from 30 minutes to 8" into "significantly improved productivity."

## Change

- AI patterns from `ai-patterns.md`.
- Errors, repetition, unclear passages.
- Generic throat-clearing that delays the point.
- Weak verb phrases, passive constructions with no human subject, abstract nouns standing in for a concrete thing.

## The two failures to check before returning

1. **Invented claims.** Anything the writer did not say: examples, numbers, opinions, certainty they did not have.
2. **Compressed voice.** Every paragraph now the same shape, every sentence the same length, the edge sanded off. If the edited draft could have been written by anyone, undo until it could not.

## When the voice itself is the problem

Sometimes the "voice" is a model's voice: the draft came from a chatbot and the user wants it to sound like them. Ask for one paragraph the user wrote themselves, or a description of how they talk, and edit toward that. Do not invent a persona.
